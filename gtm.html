GTM engineering: what we own, and what to ask their dev team

Go-to-market engineering is the plumbing between demand and revenue: the tracking, capture, routing, automation and reporting that make a marketing dollar traceable to a booked athlete. It is the layer most sports facilities never build, and it is exactly what LNL already runs.

The six workstreams

01 Tracking and attributionGA4 events and conversions, Meta CAPI, Google Ads conversions, UTM standard, server-side where possibleEvery waitlist and booking has a source, campaign and sport attached

One clarification worth having early: if they mean the Google Tag Manager container specifically, that is a small piece of workstream 01, not the whole job. Worth confirming which one they mean in the first two minutes, because the two answers lead to very different conversations.

What we need from their dev team

  • Stack inventory: what PlateOps runs on, what the signup hook calls, where the waitlist actually lives, and whether Coach Skip writes to a database or only to a phone.
  • Event schema: the names and payloads they already fire, so we extend rather than duplicate.
  • Source of truth: which system owns a lead record, and who can write to it. This decides everything downstream.
  • Access: Shopify admin and theme, GA4 and Search Console, Meta Business and pixel, Google Ads, domain DNS, CRM, booking calendar.
  • Constraints: what they refuse to change, and any timeline tied to opening.

How this differs from a marketing hire

A marketer buys attention. This builds the machine that converts attention into booked athletes and tells you which dollar did it. It is the reason a 1,000-person waitlist becomes 130 members instead of a list nobody called.

Questions to ask tonight

  • Which systems are already paid for, and which are still being chosen?
  • Where does a lead live today, and who is allowed to change that record?
  • What does Coach Skip do with a conversation it cannot answer?
  • Is the opening date fixed, and what has to be true the week before?
  • Who on their side owns the data after we build it?

Positioning line: "You already have the product, the coaches and the data. What you do not have yet is the system that turns interest into booked athletes and tells you which dollar did it. That is what I build."
