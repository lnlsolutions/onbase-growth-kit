01 · Outreach architecture

Two sports, three audiences, one pipeline.

Every conversation lands in the same place with the same fields, whether it started with a coach, a parent, or a partner org.

Team decision makers

Club and travel org directors, head coaches, high school and rec league coaches. One conversation can bring 12 to 18 athletes at once.

Private session families

Parents of athletes 6 to 21 looking for hitting, pitching, fielding, or strength work. One athlete, repeat revenue, highest margin.

Feeder partners

Rec leagues, city parks and rec, tournaments, school athletic departments, and local equipment or apparel shops.

02 · Team outreach

How a coach becomes a roster.

Team outreach is not marketing. It is a 14-day, five-touch sequence with a free assessment night as the close.

Build the target list first, in this order

1. Club and travel orgs

Every 10U through 18U org in Mesa, Gilbert, Chandler, Queen Creek, Higley, Apache Junction and Scottsdale. One row per org with director name, sport, age groups, and home field.

2. High school programs

Head coach and athletic director at each East Valley school. Approach in December, before spring practice, with a free team data night.

3. Rec leagues and city programs

Parks and rec departments, Little League and ASA charters. Slower, but they hand you the whole family base at once.

4. Tournament and showcase operators

Sponsor a cage or a skills station. You meet hundreds of families in a weekend with zero cold outreach.

One athlete at a time, on a five-minute clock.

Three lists we work every week

No-shows

Called within one hour, rebooked within 48. This list converts better than new leads.

Assessed, not sold

Every athlete who got a report and did not buy. Reached at day 30 with their own data.

Past sessions

Anyone who trained once and stopped. A text with a coach note brings a surprising number back.

Private sessions carry the highest margin and the fastest feedback. If the five-minute reply slips, everything downstream leaks.

Rules that keep the data honest

Every lead carries five fields

Source, campaign, sport, ZIP, owner. If a lead arrives without them, the form is broken and gets fixed that day.

UTM convention

source / medium / campaign / content. No exceptions, no free text, no guessing which ad produced what.

One tracking number

A dedicated number for LSA and one for the site, so calls are attributed instead of claimed.

CRM beats the platform

Ads report clicks. The CRM reports booked assessments and revenue. Decisions use the CRM.

05 · Spend tracking

Every dollar has a job and a verdict.

Spend is tracked weekly by channel and by stage, not by month-end totals. Budget is set at kickoff and adjusted on evidence.

The weekly spend ledger

ColumnWhat goes in itWhy it matters
PlannedChannel budget for the weekPacing against the 60-day plan
ActualPulled from the platform every MondayCatches overspend before it compounds
LeadsForm, call, and text inquiries from the CRMPlatform numbers flatter themselves, CRM does not
Cost per leadActual divided by CRM leadsThe single number that decides scale or cut
BookedAssessments on the calendarSeparates cheap junk leads from cheap good leads
Cost per bookingActual divided by bookedWhat the front desk should optimize
MembersClosed from that channelPayback per channel
VerdictScale, hold, or cutForces a decision every Monday

Decision rules, written before launch

Scale

Cost per booking at or under target for two weeks running, and show rate above 70 percent. Increase by 25 percent, not double.

Hold

Leads are cheap but bookings are not. Change the creative or the landing page before changing the budget.

Cut

Cost per booking above twice target after two weeks and a creative refresh. Move the budget to the winner.
