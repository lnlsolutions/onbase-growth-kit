The rule that keeps the pace honest

A signup is not a name on a list. A signup is a parent who gave a phone number, received a text within five minutes, and has a date on the calendar. If those three things are not happening, spend stops until they are.

Why the softball page ships in week one

ONBASE already coaches softball. The gap is visibility, so the page, the ad set and the partner list all target softball families from the first day of spend rather than after baseball is optimized. Demand that already exists in the East Valley is the cheapest signup available.

Decision gate, end of week two

Any channel above the blended cost per signup target by more than half gets cut and the budget moves to the cheapest lane. Any channel that books assessments at twice the rate of the others gets the next dollar. No exceptions for channels that feel good.

Phase 03 · Dec 15 – Dec 31

Opening: convert attention into a full first month.

The doors open and the market finds out. This phase is built around a founding member window with a real deadline, an event that creates content, and a referral loop that starts working the day the first athletes walk in.

Booking flow, tightened

  • Assessment slots published two weeks ahead, visible on the calendar without a phone call
  • Confirmation text immediately, reminder text 24 hours out, second reminder 2 hours out
  • No-show gets a text within the hour offering two replacement times
  • Every assessment ends with a written next step: membership recommendation, program fit, or a follow-up date

What the front desk does every single day

Morning, 20 minutes

  • Clear overnight texts and form fills
  • Confirm today's assessments and send reminders
  • Check yesterday's no-shows and offer replacements

Afternoon, 20 minutes

  • Log every conversation outcome in the CRM
  • Call any signup over 48 hours old with no booking
  • Send review request to yesterday's families

The number that matters in this phase

Show rate, not signup count. A waitlist of 800 that shows at 30% loses to a waitlist of 600 that shows at 60%. Everything in this phase is built to protect the show rate.

Handover, so it keeps running

What ONBASE owns

  • Posting cadence and coach-led content, twice weekly
  • Partner relationships and assessment nights
  • Front desk follow-up discipline, daily
  • Review requests after every second session

What stays with LNL

  • Paid media management and creative refresh
  • Local search, page and GBP optimization
  • Monthly scorecard and channel decisions
  • New program launches as they are added

What happens after January 18

The waitlist stops being the goal and retention becomes it. The same system flips to filling open class slots, reviving lapsed members and launching the next program. The dashboard stays the same, the targets change.
